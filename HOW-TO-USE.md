# Lifetime Spending Power Calculator — How to Use

For Shannon and Craig. No software to install. Open the link, use it, print.

---

## Opening it

Go to your bookmarked link. That's it — it works on a computer, tablet or phone.

There is nothing to download and nothing to sign into.

---

## The green bar at the top

Every time you open the tool, check the coloured bar at the top of the page.

| Colour | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | Government figures are current | Carry on |
| **Amber** | Figures expire within 60 days | Plan to update them soon |
| **Red** | Figures are out of date | Update before producing client reports — see below |

If the bar is red and you produce a report anyway, the report itself will carry a
visible warning saying the figures may be out of date. That is deliberate.

---

## Running a plan

1. Fill in the client's details on the left, and the assumptions on the right.
2. Press **Calculate Spending Power**.
3. After the first calculation, the numbers update automatically as you change any input —
   you don't need to press the button again.

**The big navy number** is the answer: the after-tax amount, in today's dollars, the client
can spend every year without running out by their planned age.

### Questions to ask the client

The tool prompts you where a client figure is needed rather than an estimate:

- **Pension** — the annual amount from their most recent pension statement, and the start age
- **CPP** — will they get the *full* CPP? Most clients don't. If not, use the actual figure
  from their Service Canada Statement of Contributions
- **OAS** — whether to include it, and whether the clawback applies

### Other income and expenses

Eight spare lines, four taxable and four not. Give each a label — "Rental income",
"Long-term care" — and enter the annual amount. **Positive for income, negative for an
expense.** Only lines you've labelled with a non-zero amount appear in the results.

### Target income period

Use this when a client needs a set amount for part of their retirement — for example
$200,000 a year for ten years of travel. The tool funds that first, then works out a level
spending amount for the remaining years.

## Looking at the year-by-year numbers

**Show year-by-year cash flow** opens the full table.

The **green column, "Indexed Spending Power"**, is what the client can spend that year.
Every other column adds up to it: all income, less tax, equals the green figure. The tool
checks this automatically and confirms it under the results.

**Switch to condensed view** gives a simpler table. Use the detailed view for your own
working; the client report prints the condensed one by default.

---

## Producing the client report

1. Press **Create client report**
2. Press **Print / Save as PDF**
3. In the print box that appears, choose **Save as PDF** as the destination

**Back to calculator** (or the Esc key) returns you to your figures.

Every report carries a **plan reference number** like `CSIC-20260814-01-9RFE`. Because you may
run several scenarios for one client in a day, this is how you tell them apart. Record it on
the Notes and Assumptions memorandum for that client, since the report itself does not list
the inputs.

The reference number stays the same while you adjust figures, and only changes when you press
**Calculate Spending Power** again.

---

## Saving your work

- **Save scenario** — stores the current client's figures under a name
- The dropdown reloads any saved scenario
- **Delete** removes the selected one
- **Start new client** clears the form and begins fresh

Your work is also saved automatically as you go, so closing the browser by accident won't
lose it.

**Important:** saved scenarios live on *the computer you're using*. They are not shared
between your two machines, and they aren't a backup. Anything that matters belongs in the
client file.

---

## "Check these inputs"

If an amber box appears above the results, the tool has spotted something that looks wrong —
a return below inflation, a pension starting before the client's current age, and so on.
It's a prompt to double-check, not an error.

---

## Updating the government figures

Once a year, usually January. See `MAINTENANCE.md`, or ask Carolyn.

---

## If something looks wrong

If the reconciliation message under the results ever turns **red**, stop and report it.
It means the year-by-year numbers no longer add up, and the projection shouldn't be relied on.
